1. **Note — the P3 receiver matches the encoder and wire format.**

   The mask `0xF8`, ordered bits `{8,16,32,64,128}`, base 31, and 29-bit/six-digit block are correct. `digit_of()` produces digits 0–30; its caller excludes an empty subset. Crucially, even an invalid block cannot overflow before rejection: the largest six-digit accumulator is **`31^6−1 = 887,503,680 < UINT32_MAX`**. Values at or above `2^29 = 536,870,912` are then rejected. Partial-block digit counts also fit safely.    Header selection and restrictions are correct: P3 preamble → P3 header → only P3 payload; P1 preamble still permits P1/P2; P0 permits only P0. The profile field remains two bits, sufficient for all four profiles. 2. **Note — timing is correct, with intentional compatibility and hold changes.**

   Feed continues acquisition only at `<150 ms`; polling abandons it at `>=150 ms`. Synced sections accept exactly 500 ms and reject 501 ms. Lifetime accepts 12,000 ms and rejects greater ages, measured from the **first preamble transition**, not the anchor. Unsigned subtraction handles ordinary 32-bit timer wrap correctly. [lockchan.c:158](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/lockchan.c:158), [lockchan.c:180](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/lockchan.c:180), [lockchan.c:203](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/lockchan.c:203), [lockchan.c:237](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/lockchan.c:237).

   Twelve seconds remains sensible for occasional hiccups: maximum P0 takes approximately 8.62 seconds between its first and last writes at 31 ms spacing; maximum P3 takes 2.91 seconds. It deliberately does **not** promise success when every transition takes 500 ms.

   An abandoned synced frame now holds busy about **350 ms longer**, until the first poll past 500 ms. Scroll then has its normal 1.5-second settling delay. A progressing candidate remains bounded by 12 seconds, but successive frames can still suppress settling indefinitely. P3 also invokes this hold, despite not carrying Scroll, so frequent P3 traffic can delay a genuine Scroll update. That is an existing unconditional hold policy, not a decoding failure. [lockchan.c:254](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/lockchan.c:254), [indicators.c:81](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/indicators.c:81), [indicators.c:308](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/indicators.c:308).

   P0/P1/P2 encoding is unchanged, but timing compatibility changes: a formerly valid 100–149 ms initial quiet interval now fails, as does exactly 150 ms between preamble edges. Widening `DATA_BITS` also means padding-bit activity now interrupts acquisition or its quiet interval. Once synced, unused bits remain ignored except that they can expose an already-expired gap. [lockchan.c:174](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/lockchan.c:174), [lockchan.c:197](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/lockchan.c:197).

3. **Note — waiting beyond the gap before warm-up is the right simple recovery rule.**

   The receiver already recovers itself through polling, or through the next data-capable transition after expiry. The sender must allow that expiry before transmitting a new acquisition sequence. Otherwise warm-up and preamble transitions can be legal digits of the unfinished section; `08 → 00` can even complete an old, CRC-valid P3 trailer. [lockchan.c:203](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/lockchan.c:203), [lockchan.c:221](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/lockchan.c:221).

   There is no obvious cheaper, unambiguous receiver-only reset under this alphabet. Recognizing a preamble inside an active section can discard legitimate data: the same transitions are legal digits, including after a tolerated hiccup. A reserved reset sequence or parallel candidate decoder would add protocol or implementation complexity.

   For the daemon, enforce this using **serialized write completions**, with margin rather than an exact 500 ms sleep. Already elapsed idle time counts; a song change minutes later does not incur another mandatory half-second wait. Without an acknowledgement, a lost title remains stale until refresh/repetition; coalesce queued state and resend the latest title periodically. Clock messages should identify time at the anchor so decoding delay does not become clock error. Publication and clock semantics are correctly still deferred. [plan:215](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/plans/LOCK-LED-CHANNEL-PLAN.md:215), [plan:262](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/plans/LOCK-LED-CHANNEL-PLAN.md:262).

Rejected bytes leave `host_leds` unchanged; accepted padding-clear bytes change Compose/Kana while preserving the sender’s low three bits. The board’s LED callback uses Caps/Scroll and does not drive Compose/Kana. QMK still notices whole-byte changes and runs the callback, so these writes are not entirely invisible to the core.

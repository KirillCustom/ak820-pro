4. **Fine — lighting policy; no ordinary threshold flapping.**

   After `power_task()` evaluates the supply and level:

   | Selected mode | Battery, above 150‰ or unknown level | Battery, ≤150‰ | USB |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Always on | Always-on bulb/ladder | Saver bulb/lighting ladder | No bulb; ACTIVE |
   | Balanced | Balanced bulb/ladder | Saver bulb/lighting ladder | No bulb; ACTIVE |
   | Power saver | Saver bulb/ladder | Saver bulb/ladder | No bulb; ACTIVE |

   This matches the `max(selected stage, Saver stage)` logic. The getter itself returns the selected mode on USB, **not Full**, but its display caller correctly excludes USB. It represents lighting policy, not instantaneous brightness or sleep eligibility: low-capped Always on still refuses deep sleep; Minimal retains its message backlight exception. 5. **Note — the tile is small, but the flag is not a whole-pass budget.**

   At the configured 24 MHz, 390 payload bytes take **130 µs of ideal wire time**, or about **134 µs including the 11-byte window**. Growth adds 54 bytes—18 µs of ideal wire time—and no extra window or payload chunk. Actual CPU/ChibiOS cost is much larger: the documented 12×12 RAM icon measured 3.4 ms, so a few milliseconds is the appropriate expectation, not 0.134 ms. The new tile has not been measured here. [lcd_bus.c:87](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/graphics/lcd_bus.c:87), [docs/display.md:184](/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/docs/display.md:184).

   The plan’s “separate passes” assumption is incorrect. Fn+B’s **glyphs** are queued, but retiring text/icon regions and shortening strings clear synchronously. That work, lock changes and the bulb can share one housekeeping pass. The battery flag only separates battery components. Consequently, the existing 11–12-ms plug measurements support the approach but do not prove every new Fn+B combination stays below 25 ms. A coincident 20–22-ms Caps paint has particularly little margin. Keep the planned music/Fn+B/plug/restore/wake gate, including Fn and Caps transitions; there is no demonstrated new keystroke-loss regression from static inspection.

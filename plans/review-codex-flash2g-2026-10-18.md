For **measurement interpretation**, width and repeats are answerable as *what reached this parser through the complete host-to-board path*. Closest physical arrival spacing is not directly measured:

- Parse timestamps include scheduling delay and millisecond quantization. Buffered frames can yield zero gaps; the 64-byte drain cap can also spread a backlog over multiple passes.
- Missing frames can mean host suppression, module coalescing, UART loss, or checksum rejection. Compare existing UART-error and malformed-frame counters before/after; those are trend counters, not exact loss counts.
- The additive checksum can also admit accidental matches.
- A high byte arriving masked establishes end-to-end masking, but does not uniquely locate masking inside the module without evidence of what the host actually transmitted.

These limitations are already visible in the parser and diagnostics; the trace does not conceal them, but its output alone cannot distinguish them.
